# Game Vision — The Hollow Hearth

## Player fantasy
Own a welcoming tavern in a living fantasy world, venture beyond its doors on your own terms, discover strange ingredients and friendly or dangerous supernatural beings, and gradually make the tavern and yourself more capable.

## Pillars
1. **Freedom of rhythm:** Adventure, cook, fish, gather, tend plants or open the tavern whenever desired. The game never demands a fixed daily opening.
2. **Adventure feeds hospitality:** Expeditions produce ingredients, recipes, discoveries and materials; tavern earnings and upgrades make the next expedition richer.
3. **Comfort and wonder first:** Colorful forests, sunny meadows, magical creatures, warm interiors and lively guests. Eerie ruins and hostile areas are occasional contrast, not constant horror.
4. **Tactile, readable interactions:** Gathering, cooking and serving should feel responsive without repetitive compulsory minigames.
5. **Meaningful progression:** Better equipment changes what players can reach or gather; tavern improvements enable new cooking and service possibilities, not only bigger numbers.
6. **Solo-developer feasibility:** A complete, repeatable 10–15 minute vertical slice comes before broad content.

## Core loop
Explore a compact region → gather ingredients and materials → return to the tavern → prepare dishes → choose when to open → serve supernatural customers → earn currency and experience → buy a tool or tavern upgrade → discover new opportunities.

## Tone
Cozy fantasy adventure with supernatural wonder, atmospheric mystery and occasional danger. A ghost may be a cheerful regular; a vampire may be a polite traveler. Avoid equating supernatural with horror.

## Visual identity
Detailed, cohesive HD pixel art. 2D top-down / three-quarter view, rectangular tile-based environments, non-isometric; four-direction characters; clear collision and Y-sorting; bright daytime and warm tavern contrasted with twilight mystery. Ashbound's previously approved character work is a **quality benchmark**, not a character design to copy. Art assets must be inspected in Godot at native gameplay scale.

## Boundaries
Not a hardcore business simulator, mandatory daily shift game, large scripted narrative, or survival horror game. Long-term possibilities (farming, fishing, combat, multiple biomes) are **not** initial vertical-slice commitments.

## Open decisions
Final title clearance; Godot minor version; native viewport, tile and sprite dimensions; economy and level curves; exact cooking interaction; asset sourcing and licensing. Resolve through prototypes and playtests, not guesswork.
